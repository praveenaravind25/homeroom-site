The story so far.

What shipped, and what changed in the kitchen. Newest first.

  1. November 2, 2026

    No more dark ovals behind the clock

    The clock and what's next used to sit in dark ovals in the corners. On real photos they looked like smudges, so they're gone. A soft gradient now runs along the bottom, and every letter carries its own shadow, so the photo stays the photo.

    homeroom #104
  2. November 2, 2026

    Recipes that end in a grocery list

    A recipe shared from a phone lands in a recipe box on the wall, scaled the way a cookbook would, with anything the AI guessed flagged for you to keep or discard. The week's plan then becomes one shopping list, grouped by aisle, and only identical items merge, so light mayonnaise stays apart from mayonnaise. Lock in sends it to the shared Groceries list, so it's on both phones, and a midweek swap sends only the new lines.

    homeroom #85, #97, #87, #102
  3. November 2, 2026

    Who's at the door: the wall answers

    Cameras now sits in the Rail, one big view and two smaller ones, and any of them opens full screen, with the porch light a tap away and unlock behind a deliberate hold. When someone rings the doorbell, the wall opens that same view on its own, whatever it was showing, even a dark Night screen. Close it, or leave it alone for about a minute, and it puts back exactly what was there before. The whole path is tested end to end with a fake doorbell, so nobody had to stand on the porch pressing the button.

    homeroom #71, #98, #54
  4. November 2, 2026

    Real data on the wall

    The fakes came off. Weather, who's home, lights and locks now come from Home Assistant, the week comes from the family calendars, and chores, meals and lists come from Family Hub. Photos were the last to move: the wall can now read the family's own Immich library, and anything hidden or archived there stays off the screen. Each one plugs in behind the same seam the fakes used, so the screens didn't have to change.

    homeroom #25, #26, #35, #36, #41
  5. November 1, 2026

    The photo steps aside, and the Rail opens

    The first try kept the photo on screen and raised Today over it. After a house test on the tablet, the household picked a different idea: a touch means you want something, so the photo yields. It shrinks into the rail, and Today, Week, Home, Chores and who's home are one tap away. Quick add uses picks instead of a keyboard, because nobody types on a wall.

    homeroom #10, #18, #27, #33, #38, #39, #40
  6. November 1, 2026

    A photo frame that chooses well

    Before it could be a calendar, it had to be a good photo frame. Curation balances people and years, skips screenshots, receipts and blurry shots, and can say in plain words why it picked a photo. Photos fill the screen edge to edge with a slow drift, and the wall moves between Ambient, Glance and Night instead of sitting in one state.

    homeroom #12, #13, #15, #16
  7. October 31, 2026

    Foundation: a server, a tablet and a house style

    Homeroom started as one small server at home with a fake behind every source, so the screens could be built before any real data showed up. It ran on the real wall tablet from the first day, at its true 1263×841. Then came the house style: Schibsted Grotesk, a warm dark ground, and brass only for what's happening now.

    homeroom #4, #9, #11
